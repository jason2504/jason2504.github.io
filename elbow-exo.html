Elbow Exoskeleton

Key Achievement

Developed a 1-DOF elbow exoskeleton (0–115° ROM, ~1100g) with multi-modal sensor fusion integrating EMG, IMU, and motion capture in a comparative ablation study to quantify adaptive rehabilitation assistance. Features a CubeMars AK45-36 BLDC motor (15 Nm continuous, 24 Nm peak), a print-in-place snap-fit forearm linkage passively compensating for the ~8mm elbow COR shift, multi-size TPU orthotics (R35/40/45 forearm; R45/50/55 upper arm) with PLA structural inserts resisting torsional twist, and dual hardware and software safety stops. Available in both left and right arm versions.

🦾 Ongoing research at the University of Pennsylvania GRASP Laboratory under Dr. Nadia Figueroa.

Project Overview

Upper limb exoskeletons for repetitive industrial tasks must adapt to time-varying human dynamics: muscle fatigue, impedance changes, and overlapping kinematic phases during lifting. Single-modality sensing leads to state misclassification and suboptimal assistance. This project develops a sensor fusion framework integrating EMG, IMU, and motion capture to robustly estimate user state throughout repetitive lifting tasks. Through comparative controller evaluation and ablation studies, we quantify how multi-modal sensing improves adaptive control performance and enables more reliable, responsive elbow assistance.

Research Approach: Ablation Study

The core research contribution is a comparative ablation study evaluating three controllers — EMG-only (baseline), IMU-only (baseline), and fully fused EMG+IMU+MoCap (proposed). A Kalman filter fuses these asynchronous streams into a unified state estimate: joint angle and velocity, normalized muscle activation level, fatigue index (0 = fresh to 1 = exhausted computed from EMG median frequency shift and RMS amplitude decay), and estimated joint impedance. The adaptive controller then selects torque profiles based on detected motion phase (rest/lift/lower), fatigue level, and estimated impedance. Evaluation metrics: assistance timing accuracy, torque tracking error, false positive rate, and fatigue adaptation quality.

Mechanical Design

Left & Right Arm Versions

The exoskeleton is available in two mirrored versions — left and right — accommodating both limbs with the user in a supinated (palms-upward) position. The motor sits at the elbow joint. The forearm linkage assembly is identical across both versions; only the upper arm linkage, upper arm orthotic, and forearm orthotic differ (mirrored CAD). Both versions are fully documented in the GitHub repository with separate SolidWorks assembly files for each side.

Structural Linkages

Two structural linkages bolt directly onto the CubeMars AK45-36 motor using M3 screws: the upper arm linkage (~270 × 110 × 24 mm) and the forearm linkage assembly (~210 × 35 × 19 mm). Each linkage features 6–7 orthotic mounting holes spaced 21mm apart, providing ~126mm of total adjustment range to accommodate a broad range of adult arm proportions. Wall thicknesses and cross-sections were sized to safely transmit the actuator's 15 Nm continuous torque with adequate bending and torsional safety margins. Both linkages are printed at 75% infill with 4 perimeter wall loops — 75% infill provides near-maximum mechanical performance, with diminishing structural returns beyond ~80% infill.

Snap-Fit Forearm Extension

During elbow flexion and extension, the human elbow's center of rotation shifts by approximately 8mm due to the cam-like geometry of the distal humerus. A rigid forearm linkage would resist this shift, creating uncomfortable shear forces on the user's arm. The forearm linkage is split into two pieces — a base and a front — joined by a print-in-place snap-fit. This passively accommodates the COR shift throughout the full range of motion without any user adjustment. The snap-fit is printed in a single bed using bridging with supports, requiring no post-print machining.

Orthotics & Structural Inserts

Both orthotics are printed in TPU for compliance and comfort. Three size options per orthotic cover a broad range of adult arm circumferences: R35/R40/R45mm inner radius for the forearm (slightly tapered to follow natural forearm geometry) and R45/R50/R55mm for the upper arm. Each orthotic features 6 shoelace holes per side for secure tightening and 6mm EVA foam bonded to the inner surface with Barge contact cement for pressure distribution.

A critical design feature is the PLA structural insert embedded at each orthotic-to-linkage interface. Without it, the flexible TPU twists under motor torque, misaligning the linkage. The 75% infill PLA inserts resist torsional loading at the attachment point while the outer TPU shell maintains user comfort. TPU orthotics are printed at 15% infill — low infill preserves compliance and keeps mass down while 4 wall loops provide sufficient shape retention; higher infill would make orthotics unnecessarily stiff without structural benefit.

The upper arm orthotic additionally includes two recessed EMG sensor slots — one positioned over the biceps and one over the triceps — to securely house the ANR Muscle Sense M40 sensors with consistent placement across sessions. Both the upper arm and forearm orthotics also incorporate a PLA structural insert seated at the orthotic-to-linkage interface; without it, the compliant TPU twists under the motor's torque and misaligns the linkage. The 75% infill PLA insert resists this torsional loading while the outer TPU shell maintains comfort against the skin.

Safety Systems

Two independent safety layers protect the user from joint over-travel. A software stop enforces joint angle limits (0° and 115°) in the motor controller firmware. A hardware mechanical stop uses M6 screw and hex nut assemblies to physically block the forearm linkage at each range limit, covered with TPU mechanical stop covers to cushion contact. The 0° stop is in a fixed hole for a firm absolute limit. The 115° stop is in a slot, adjustable up to ~140° to match individual user anatomy. This dual-layer architecture ensures protection even in the event of a software fault.

Multi-Modal Sensing & Control

Sensor Modalities

  • EMG (bicep + tricep): ANR Muscle Sense M40 sensors housed in orthotic slots. Captures muscle activation intent, fatigue via median frequency shift and RMS amplitude decay.
  • IMU (elbow joint): Joint angle, velocity, acceleration; motion phase detection (rest / lift / lower).
  • Motion Capture: High-precision kinematic ground truth for validation and ablation study evaluation.

Electronics

  • MCU: Arduino Portenta H7 (dual-core STM32H747 at 480+240 MHz)
  • M7 core: Real-time motor control via CAN bus (TJA1051 transceiver)
  • M4 core: EMG signal processing — 20–450 Hz bandpass filter, 60 Hz notch filter, 200ms RMS windows, intent classification
  • Power: 6S LiPo (22.2V), MP1584EN buck to 5V for MCU; back-mounted 650g power/control unit for wireless operation

Donning & Doffing

The exoskeleton is worn and removed as a single fully-assembled unit — the linkages, orthotics, and motor are not disassembled between uses.

Putting on: Loosen both orthotic laces fully. With the arm straight out (0°), slide the assembly onto the arm — upper arm orthotic end first — until the motor sits at the elbow joint center of rotation. Tighten both orthotics until secure but comfortable. Connect electronics.

Taking off: Power down. Loosen both laces. Slide the entire assembly off the arm, forearm end first.
