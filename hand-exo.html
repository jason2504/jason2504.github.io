Hand Exoskeleton

Key Achievement

Designed and built a compact, single-motor, bidirectional hand exoskeleton for 4-finger grip assistance targeting patients with partial voluntary hand control (post-stroke or spinal cord injury). Drives all four fingers simultaneously via a Waveshare ST3215 servo, a 2.5:1 spur gear transmission with a +0.5mm tooth depth modification for reliable 3D-printed gear mesh, and a parallel four-bar linkage per finger adapted from Esposito et al. (2022). All 17 custom parts fit on a single print bed (~11.5 hours, ~233g PLA). A physical prototype has been built and is under active hardware testing.

🖐️ Ongoing research at the University of Pennsylvania GRASP Laboratory under Dr. Nadia Figueroa.

Project Overview

The device targets patients who retain partial voluntary hand control — common in stroke recovery or incomplete spinal cord injury. Rather than fully replacing voluntary effort, it augments it by driving all four fingers simultaneously through a shared transmission shaft, keeping the device compact and lightweight enough for daily wear. The design philosophy was aggressive simplicity: one motor, one transmission, one print bed.

Grip strength analysis from published data (male average 45 kg, female average 32 kg; Leyk et al. 2007) set the target at 20 kg (200 N) of assistive grip force. With a 50mm effective lever arm at the PIP joint, this requires approximately 10 Nm at the output — met by the ST3215's 2.94 Nm stall torque amplified through the 2.5:1 gear train and the mechanical advantage of the four-bar linkage system.

The design draws from and extends Esposito et al. (2022, Machines 10(1):57), replacing a Bowden cable transmission with a direct wrist-mounted spur gear drive for a more compact form factor and fewer failure modes.

Mechanical Design

Wrist Mount & Housing

The wrist mount is the central structural element, housing all electronics in a 175mm × 100mm × 20mm bay. It integrates the left transmission mount directly into its body, provides a dedicated motor mount interface, and secures to the wrist via Velcro straps through strap channels. A curved underside matches natural wrist curvature for comfort. A separate right transmission mount slides over the shaft after installation — this split-mount design allows the shaft to be inserted laterally, avoiding threading through confined spaces.

Spur Gear Transmission

The Waveshare ST3215 serial bus servo was chosen for its 30 kg·cm (2.94 Nm) stall torque at 12V, compact 69g form factor, 360° magnetic encoder with 4096-count resolution, two-way feedback (position, load, speed, voltage), and serial daisy-chain bus supporting up to 253 servos at up to 1 Mbps.

The gear train uses a 20-tooth drive gear (M2.5-screwed directly to the motor horn) meshing with a 50-tooth driven gear (keywayed to the transmission shaft) for a 2.5:1 ratio. Both gears are Module 1.25 with tooth depth modified +0.5mm beyond the standard SolidWorks Toolbox profile. This deliberate adjustment compensates for dimensional variation in 3D-printed gears and motor mount positional offsets, ensuring proper mesh engagement without requiring precision-machined center distances. Both gears and the transmission shaft are printed at 75% infill for torsional rigidity.

Transmission Shaft & Bearings

The transmission shaft distributes torque from the large spur gear to all four finger base linkages, supported at both ends by sealed stainless steel ball bearings (5mm ID × 10mm OD × 4mm). Four base linkages mount to the shaft at 22.5mm spacing (approximating natural inter-finger spacing), connected via 18-8 stainless steel quick-release pins (McMaster-Carr). Quick-release pins allow any individual finger assembly to be removed instantly without tools — important for fitting sessions and patient-specific customization in a rehabilitation context.

Parallel Four-Bar Finger Linkage System

Each finger uses a parallel four-bar linkage topology from Esposito et al. (2022), converting rotational shaft motion into guided flexion/extension that follows natural human finger kinematics. The linkage chain per finger: base linkage (65.4mm, quick-release pin to shaft) → intermediate linkage (130mm for index/middle/ring, 100mm for pinky — shorter to match the fifth finger's length) → vertical linkage (50mm, multiple hole positions for adjustability) → base finger connector (MCP joint, curved underside, slot connection) → L-shape linkage (60mm W × 28mm L) → small finger connector (PIP/DIP joints, curved, slot connection).

Key fit features: Multiple mounting hole positions on both the intermediate and vertical linkages allow adjustment to the user's finger length. Slot connections at both finger connector interfaces provide continuous fine adjustment beyond discrete hole positions. All connections use M3 screws and nuts except where quick-release pins are specified.

Electronics & Assembly

Power & Communication

All electronics fit within the wrist mount bay. An Adafruit ESP32-S3 Feather (5V, Wi-Fi + BLE) communicates with the ST3215 via half-duplex UART through a Waveshare Bus Servo Adapter A, which also provides 12V servo power directly from a 3S LiPo battery (400–600 mAh, ~38g, XT30). A Mini360 buck converter steps 12V down to 5V for the ESP32. A decoupling capacitor should be added across the servo power rail in the final circuit to suppress voltage spikes during motor commutation.

Assembly Summary (10 Steps)

  1. Assemble all four finger linkage chains (base → intermediate → vertical → connectors)
  2. Screw small spur gear (20T) onto motor horn with M2.5 screws
  3. Mount motor in motor mount; mount assembly into wrist mount
  4. Press-fit large spur gear (50T) onto transmission shaft gap, aligning keyway
  5. Attach finger base linkages to transmission shaft at 22.5mm spacing
  6. Press bearings into mounts; slide shaft through left bearing, gear, and right bearing
  7. Screw right transmission mount to wrist mount from bottom and side
  8. Verify smooth gear mesh; adjust motor mount center distance if needed
  9. Seat electronics on standoffs in wrist mount bay; wire per diagram
  10. Secure to wrist with Velcro straps; attach finger connectors; power on and test
